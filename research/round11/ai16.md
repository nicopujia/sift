## F1113 (high): A revisit shows an old Short version when an article changed only a short line with a time, a duration or two words: 'Doors open at 19:30' stays after the page says 20:00

Found by: R3-ai-background. Where: src/background/text.ts:906 (stableText drops lines of 2 words or fewer and lines of 8 words or fewer that match TICK), used by src/background/index.ts:780 (the exact cache key) and src/background/text.ts:1249 (textLines, the round 10 reuse check); src/content/short.ts:197-200 (TICK lines of under 8 words get dfp -1, so freshUnits/staleAbout never see them)

Evidence: Chrome 9469 with my mock. fx doors.html said 'Doors open at 19:30.', and the first visit made 1 call and showed 'Doors open at 19:30.' I then changed the file to 'Doors open at 20:00.', with the same title and all other lines the same. The revisit made 0 calls and the Short version still showed 'Doors open at 19:30.', while the page said 20:00. The old sentence passes the number check because the page has '30' elsewhere ('30,000 people', 'Buses 30 and 31'). Offline (r11ai/t/stable.ts), stableText is the same before and after each of these edits: 'Pool: open'→'Pool: closed', 'Doors open at 19:30.'→'20:00.', 'Take 2 tablets every 4 hours.'→'Take 1 tablet every 6 hours.', and 'Expect delays of 20 minutes.'→'90 minutes.' The same gap shows within one visit. In fx doorslive.html a script changes the line at 9 s. The Short version then shows both 'Doors open at 19:30.' and the new paragraph, because the old line never had a dfp hash, so staleAbout cannot drop it.

Repro: Start bun scratchpad/r11ai/mock.ts (port 8882) and run SIFT_PORT=9469 SIFT_TAB=R3-ai-background bun scratchpad/r11ai/settings.ts mock. Then: cp fx/doorsA.html fx/doors.html; bun scratchpad/r11ai/watch.ts doors.html 7 'short version|Doors'; cp fx/doorsB.html fx/doors.html; bun scratchpad/r11ai/watch.ts doors.html 7 'short version|Doors'. The second run prints condense=0 and 'Doors open at 19:30.' Offline: bun scratchpad/r11ai/t/stable.ts.

Suspected cause: TICK was meant for timestamps and counters ('Updated 10:32', '5 min ago', '1,2 M views'). It also matches content lines that state a time or a duration, and the 2-word cut drops short list items ('Pool: closed'). These lines are left out of the cache key, the reuse check and the content side's change hashes. Lead: drop a line only when, after removing the time or count, it is only a label ('Updated', 'Actualizado', 'min read'). Or keep every non-link prose line in the key and in textLines.

Skeptic: Confirmed, high. I used Chrome 9469 with a copy of the reporter's mock. doors.html with 'Doors open at 19:30.' made 1 call. I then changed the file to say 20:00. The revisit made 0 calls and the Short version still said 'Doors open at 19:30.', while the page (curl) says 20:00. A worse case is mine: I added a two-word line, <p><strong>Concert cancelled.</strong></p>. The revisit made 0 calls and the Short version did not mention the cancellation. Offline, t/stable.ts shows the same cache-key text for all 4 edits. The old time passes the number check only because '30' appears elsewhere on the page. Without the other '30', checked() drops the sentence and the time is just missing. Within one visit, doorslive.html at 16 s showed both 'Doors open at 19:30.' and the new 'Doors open at 20:00. The start is delayed…'. The reuse path has the same gap: my reuse2.ts renumbers [[B1]] to [[B2]] and changes the time, and reuseShort still returns the old 19:30 text.

Skeptic's fix lead: src/background/text.ts stableText: keep every prose line that has no link in the key, including short ones. Drop a line only when it is nothing but a label once its time or count is removed ('Updated 10:32', '5 min ago', '1,2 M views'). Never drop it just for having 2 words or fewer. textLines inherits the fix. Make the same change to `counts` in src/content/short.ts, so a changed short line gets a change hash and staleAbout can drop the old sentence.

## F1114 (high, regression): A page whose lang names another language (lang="en" on a Polish, Swedish, Turkish or Basque page) now gets its Short version translated: the model is told 'The text is in English. Write in English.'

Found by: R3-ai-background. Where: src/background/index.ts:756 (condenseLang) and :839 (the user message names the language); src/background/text.ts:151 (entryLang returns the page language when the detected language has no FUNCTION_WORDS list)

Evidence: Chrome 9469 detects the Polish, Swedish and Turkish paragraphs as pl/sv/tr, reliable at 100% (scratchpad r11ai/detect.ts). entryLang(text, 'en', detect) still returns 'en' for each one, because only en/es/pt/fr/de/it/nl/ca/gl have word lists. With the mock, fx pl.html (html lang="en", Polish article) sent 'The text is in English. Write in English.' One run with Sift's default models gave a Short version in English: 'Opposition budget changes for schools and nurseries were rejected. Opposition councillors will complain to the governor…' That English text is now cached for 30 days, and condenseFits accepts it since want is 'en'. Before round 10 the prompt only said 'Same language as the text. Never translate.' A Serbian Latin page (lang sr or sr-Latn) is detected as 'bs' and told 'The text is in Bosnian. Write in Bosnian.'

Repro: With the mock: bun scratchpad/r11ai/watch.ts pl.html 8, then curl -s 127.0.0.1:8882/log shows the 'The text is in English' line. With the real chain (settings.ts real, which clears the cache), the same watch prints an English Short version under the Polish title. Offline: bun scratchpad/r11ai/t/wantlang.ts.

Suspected cause: condenseLang trusts the page's lang unless the detected language has a function-word list. Then the language is named to the model, which outranks the system prompt's 'never translate'. Lead: name the language only when entryLang agrees with a reliable detection (or the detection is unreliable). When a reliable detection of the text differs from the page's lang, name the detected language, or name none and keep 'Same language as the text'.

Skeptic: Confirmed, high, regression. In the code, entryLang(text, 'en', detect) returns the page language whenever the detected language (pl/sv/tr/id) has no FUNCTION_WORDS list. t/wantlang.ts prints want='en' for each of them. Round 10 added the prompt line 'The text is in X. Write in X.' (round10-fixes.diff). With the mock, pl.html (lang=en, Polish text) sent 'The text is in English. Write in English.' I made one real-chain run with the cache cleared. The Polish article got a Short version in English: 'After a debate lasting over six hours, opposition members accused the mayor of excessive debt…'. condenseFits(out, 'en') then accepts it, and it is cached.

Skeptic's fix lead: src/background/text.ts entryLang (as used by condenseLang in src/background/index.ts:756): when a reliable detection of the long prose differs from the page lang, return the detected language even if it has no word list. Or return '' so the prompt names no language and the 'Same language as the text' rule decides. Name a language only when the page lang and a reliable detection agree, or detection is unreliable.

## F1115 (high): The number check drops faithful sentences in languages without number words: a Polish Short version loses its lead ('budget passed 20 to 9'), the fare rise and the bus purchase

Found by: R3-ai-background. Where: src/background/text.ts:310 (NUMBER_WORDS covers only en/es/pt/fr/de/it), :580 (sourceNumbers), :824 (dropUnsupported drops the sentence)

Evidence: I captured Sift's exact condense prompt for fx pl2.html (lang="pl") with the mock, then sent it once to the last-resort model (openai/gpt-6-luna; the main model answered 402). The answer was faithful and wrote numbers as digits: 'Rada miasta przyjęła budżet na 2027 rok stosunkiem głosów 20 do 9.', 'Bilety … zdrożeją od stycznia o 10 proc.', 'Miasto chce za to kupić 40 autobusów…', '…w życie 1 stycznia…' and 'Dochody mają wynieść około 4 mld zł.' The source spells these out (dwudziestu, dziewięciu, dziesięć, czterdzieści, pierwszego, czterech). Sift's checks (r11ai/t/plcheck.ts) drop all five sentences as 'number'. Replayed through Chrome, the Short version ('KRÓTKA WERSJA') starts with 'Po debacie trwającej ponad sześć godzin opozycja zarzuciła prezydentowi…'. The vote, the fare rise and the buses are gone, and nothing puts them back. The same happens for Catalan, Dutch, Swedish, Russian and any other language whose number words are not listed.

Repro: bun scratchpad/r11ai/t/plcheck.ts (uses r11ai/last-condense.json and raw-pl.txt). In Chrome: mock running, settings.ts mock, then bun scratchpad/r11ai/watch.ts pl2.html 7.

Suspected cause: sourceNumbers knows number words in six languages only, so a digit the model writes for a Polish number word counts as invented, and the whole sentence goes. Lead: when the page language has no number-word list, do not drop a sentence for 'number' alone. Or put the source sentence back, as warningOf does for warnings.

Skeptic: Confirmed. I rate it medium to high. NUMBER_WORDS covers only en/es/pt/fr/de/it. In Chrome 9469, the mock replayed the captured real model answer for pl2.html (lang=pl). resp.json is an authentic OpenRouter gpt-6-luna response. The 'KRÓTKA WERSJA' starts at 'Po debacie…', and the vote '20 do 9', the 10% fare rise, the 40 buses, '1 stycznia' and '4 mld zł' are all dropped. t/plcheck.ts drops those 5 sentences as 'number'. The source spells them dwudziestu, dziewięciu, dziesięć, czterdzieści, pierwszego and czterech. My own real run on the lang=en Polish page (N1) lost the same lead facts: its English Short version has no vote count, fare rise or buses. How often this happens depends on how often a source spells numbers out. When it does, the article's key facts disappear silently.

Skeptic's fix lead: src/background/text.ts unsupported/dropUnsupported: when the source is in a language with no NUMBER_WORDS list, do not drop a sentence for 'number' alone. Pass the language in, or detect it by the source having number-like words that wordNumbers cannot read. Alternatively, put the source sentence back the way warningOf does, or extend NUMBER_WORDS to more languages.

## F1116 (medium, regression): stripPreamble now removes a real first or last paragraph that starts 'This is / Here is / Esta es … version / summary' (new NOTE_HANDOVER): 'This is the first version of Android that…' disappears

Found by: R3-ai-background. Where: src/background/text.ts:863 (NOTE_HANDOVER), :868 (stripPreamble)

Evidence: Chrome 9469 with the mock: fx handover.html. The model's lead is 'This is the first version of Android that lets phones send text messages by satellite, with no mobile signal.' The Short version starts with 'It also adds a new lock screen and longer battery life.' The article's main point is gone and nothing puts it back. Offline (r11ai/t/handover.ts), all 7 cases are removed: 'This is the last version of Windows 10 that gets security fixes.', 'Esta es la primera versión de Android que…', 'Here is the new version of the tax form, with three changes…', a closing 'This is the version the Senate votes on Thursday.', its Spanish form, and 'This is a summary of the council's decisions on 3 March.'

Repro: Mock and settings as above, then bun scratchpad/r11ai/watch.ts handover.html 8. Offline: bun scratchpad/r11ai/t/handover.ts.

Suspected cause: NOTE_HANDOVER treats any paragraph that opens with 'this is/here is/esta es', then up to two words and then version/summary/rewrite, as the model handing over its text. Lead: count it as a handover only when the paragraph is that phrase alone, ending in ':' or '.', with no other clause (as 'Here is the shorter version of the text:'), or when its words are not in the source.

Skeptic: Confirmed, medium, regression. NOTE_HANDOVER matches 'this is/here is/esta es' followed by up to 2 words and 'version/summary'. stripPreamble then removes that first or last paragraph. t/handover.ts removed all 7 real-content paragraphs. In Chrome with the mock, handover.html's Short version starts at 'It also adds a new lock screen…'. The article's main point, satellite texting, is gone and the full article does not come back. The trigger is narrow: the model has to open or close with that phrase. When it does, a real fact is silently removed.

Skeptic's fix lead: src/background/text.ts NOTE_HANDOVER/stripPreamble: count it as a handover only when the paragraph is just that phrase. It should end in ':' or be a single short clause with nothing after 'version/summary' (as in 'Here is the shorter version of the text:'). Or require the paragraph's words to be absent from the source.

## F1117 (medium, regression): The speaker check now accepts any person named earlier in the section for a 'he said' quote, so a quote the source gives to the person named right before it can be given to someone else

Found by: R3-ai-background. Where: src/background/text.ts:712-715 (speakerNear, pronoun branch)

Evidence: Offline (r11ai/t/spk3.ts). The source first names union leader Maria Lopez. 564 characters later it says 'The plant's director, John Smith, met reporters on Friday. “We cannot keep every job if the orders do not come back,” he said.' The rewrite 'Union leader Maria Lopez said “we cannot keep every job if the orders do not come back”.' gets unsupported() = '' and is kept. That gives the director's words to the union leader on the other side. Before round 10, the 300-character window rejected it as 'speaker'.

Repro: bun scratchpad/r11ai/t/spk3.ts prints 'ok (kept)' for the misattributed sentence.

Suspected cause: The F1017 fix accepts the named speaker anywhere before a pronoun-attributed quote in its section. Lead: accept a person only when no other person (a name or a role) is introduced between that mention and the quote, as John Smith is here.

Skeptic: Confirmed, medium, regression. t/spk3.ts gives unsupported() = '' (kept) for 'Union leader Maria Lopez said “we cannot keep every job…”'. In the source, John Smith is introduced in the sentence right before the 'he said' quote. In the code, the round 10 pronoun branch of speakerNear accepts any named person from the start of the section up to the quote. Before round 10, the ±300-character window would have rejected Lopez, who is 564 characters away. The check can now pass a model's misattribution to an earlier-named person, which is exactly what it exists to catch. It needs a model error to show up.

Skeptic's fix lead: src/background/text.ts speakerNear, the saidByPronoun branch: find the last person (speakerWords name or role) named before the quote in the section. Accept the rewrite's speaker only if it is that person, or if no other person is named between the speaker's mention and the quote.

## F1118 (medium, regression): A Short version that fails the language check twice is not remembered: every visit pays two model calls and shows no Short version (for example a Spanish paragraph made mostly of English product names)

Found by: R3-ai-background. Where: src/background/index.ts:853-857 (second language failure returns a final error and caches nothing); :763 (condenseFits per paragraph)

Evidence: Chrome 9469 with the mock: fx teches.html (lang es). The mock's Spanish Short version has the paragraph 'Las novedades incluyen Apple Intelligence, Live Translation for AirPods, Liquid Glass design and the new Games app for iPhone.' Chrome detects that paragraph as en (reliable, 100%), so condenseFits fails. Visit 1 made 2 condense calls and showed the full text. Visit 2 made 2 more and again showed no Short version. pten.html, where the mock always answers in English, also makes 2 calls on every visit. Other final errors (a filter, an output limit, 'not much shorter') are saved as NO_SHORT for a day. Milder mixes ('…Apple Intelligence en más idiomas, Live Translation for AirPods, el diseño Liquid Glass…') are detected as es and pass, so the trigger is a paragraph made mostly of English names.

Repro: Mock and settings as above, then bun scratchpad/r11ai/watch.ts teches.html 8 twice. Each run prints condense=2 and no 'Versión corta'.

Suspected cause: The language-retry path returns before the NO_SHORT save. Lead: on the second language failure, cacheSet(key, NO_SHORT, min(ttl, DAY)) as the other final errors do. Also skip paragraphs whose words are mostly capitalized names when checking each paragraph.

Skeptic: Confirmed, medium, regression. In Chrome 9469 with the mock, teches.html made condense=2 and showed no 'Versión corta' on both visits. pten.html (the model always answers in English) also made condense=2 on each of 2 visits. In the code, the second condenseFits failure in doCondense returns a final error inside the try, before any cacheSet. Other final failures (finalIncomplete, tooLong) save NO_SHORT for a day. So every visit pays two calls for nothing. The teches trigger is a paragraph made mostly of English product names, which is uncommon. The persistent-translation case, the F1035 scenario, is not uncommon.

Skeptic's fix lead: src/background/index.ts doCondense, the language-retry branch (`if (again) return reply(...)`): before returning, call `if (useCache) cacheSet(key, NO_SHORT, Math.min(ttlOf(req, pc) ?? DAY, DAY))`. Optionally, in condenseFits, skip paragraphs whose words are mostly capitalized names.
