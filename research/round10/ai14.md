## F1014 (high, regression): A Short version saved for an earlier text of the same article is reused when the article has changed: it states a claim the page now contradicts, keeps a paragraph that was removed, loses new short paragraphs, and puts a link on another link's address

Found by: R3-ai-background. Where: src/background/text.ts:974-983 (reuseShort coverage check); src/background/index.ts:760-773 (reuse path); src/content/ai.ts:476-482 (meta taken from the current text, so planShort shows nothing in full)

Evidence: Chrome 9469 with my mock model on port 8881. Two fixtures share one h1 on one site, as an edited article would. mayor1.html made 1 condense call, and its Short version starts 'Mayor Ana Ruiz said on Monday that she will run for a third term…'. mayor2.html says 'she will not run'. It made 0 calls and showed the same 'she will run' sentence, and nothing put the changed paragraph back. mayor5.html removes the car-thefts paragraph. It made 0 calls, and the Short version still says 'Police reported fewer car thefts this.' mayor3.html adds a 'Road closures on Sunday:' list with items under 8 words. The Short version has none of it. guide2.html removes the passport paragraph. Its Short version still says 'Renew your passport online here', and that link now goes to /report-lost-card (linkcheck.ts prints the href). The same happens offline with reuseShort (r10ai/t/reuse.ts, reuselink.ts). Cause: reuse needs only 95% of the new text's 8-word-or-longer lines (by words) to appear in the old text. Shorter lines never count, and lines the old text had but the new one lacks are never checked. Link ids are renumbered by their words alone. The reused text is checked only for quotes and numbers. It is then saved under the new key for 30 days. The page gets meta for the current text, so freshUnits is empty and the changed or new paragraphs are not shown in full, as they are after a change within one visit. Inference, not tested: daily pages updated at one URL (puzzle hints, schedules) would show yesterday's facts.

Repro: Start the mock: bun /tmp/claude-1001/-home-nico/aac11194-acaf-4dad-a334-0620115b1898/scratchpad/r10ai/mock.ts, in tmux. Then run SIFT_PORT=9469 SIFT_TAB=R3-ai-background bun scratchpad/r10ai/settings.ts. Then, in this order: bun scratchpad/r10ai/watch.ts mayor1.html 8 'short version|Mayor Ana' and then mayor2.html, mayor5.html 'car thefts' and mayor3.html 'Main Street'. For the link: bun scratchpad/r10ai/linkcheck.ts guide1.html, then guide2.html. Offline: bun scratchpad/r10ai/t/reuse.ts and t/reuselink.ts.

Suspected cause: reuseShort treats 'the new text's long lines are 95% inside the old text' as 'the same text'. Fix lead: reuse only when every prose unit of both texts matches (short ones included), or return the old text with the reuse so the page shows new or changed paragraphs in full and drops sentences about gone ones (planShort already does this within a visit). Map a link only when its line is unchanged.

Skeptic: Confirmed, high. Chrome 9469 with the mock: mayor1 made 1 condense call. Then mayor2 made 0 calls, and its Short version opens 'she will run for a third term' while the page says 'will not run'. Repeated in the other order after a cache clear: mayor3 first (1 call, its Short version has 'Road closures on Sunday. Main Street is closed all day.'), then mayor2 (0 calls) showed 'will run' and 'Road closures… Main Street is closed all day.' The mayor2 page has neither. mayor5 (car-thefts paragraph removed) made 0 calls and still showed 'Police reported fewer car thefts this.' mayor3 reusing mayor1's version showed no road closures, while a fresh mayor3 call keeps them. guide1 then guide2: 0 calls, and 'Renew your passport online here' links to /report-lost-card (guide2 has no passport paragraph). The offline runs of reuse.ts and reuselink.ts agree. Cause, from the code: reuseShort (text.ts ~968) counts only the 8-word-or-longer lines of the new text and wants 95% of their words in the old text. A one-word edit to one line of a 25-line article passes. Lines the old text had and the new one lacks are never checked. A link is mapped by its words alone when only one candidate is left (`same.length === 1`). index.ts ~762-770 then saves the result under the new key, and the content side builds meta from the current text, so freshUnits is empty and nothing is shown in full.

Skeptic's fix lead: src/background/text.ts reuseShort: reuse only when the prose line sets match both ways, short lines included (or allow only lines the new text adds at the end, as with lazy loading). Map a link only when `y.line === x.line`, and drop the `same.length === 1` fallback. Better: have doCondense (src/background/index.ts reuse branch) return p.text with the reply, and build the content's ShortMeta from that text (src/content/ai.ts condense). planShort then shows changed or new paragraphs in full and drops sentences about removed ones, as it does within one visit.

Found by: R3-ai-background. Where: src/background/text.ts:656-673 (NOTE_START 'i (have|kept|left|will…)', i've, 'he mantenido', note/nota; NOTE_TOPIC version/article/words/text/links); called from src/background/index.ts:812 and 841

The mock's first paragraph is 'I have tested every version of the Pixel phone since 2016, and the Pixel 11 is the first one I would not recommend.' The Short version starts with 'The design is almost the same as last year's.' The verdict is gone, and nothing puts it back. Offline (r10ai/t/pre3.ts, pre2.ts), these are removed too: 'I've used this app for five years, and the new version breaks the one feature I need.', 'I left Twitter after twelve years; this article explains why.', 'I kept a diary … the words I wrote most…', 'He mantenido este blog … esta es la versión que más me gusta.' and 'I will not vote for this budget … the text of the housing law…'. A last paragraph is removed in the same way: 'Note: This article was corrected on March 3 to fix the date of the vote.', 'Nota: este artículo fue actualizado con la cifra oficial de heridos.' and 'Here are the links to the forms you need to apply.'

Repro: Offline: cd /home/nico/projects/sift-test-9 && bun /tmp/claude-1001/-home-nico/aac11194-acaf-4dad-a334-0620115b1898/scratchpad/r10ai/t/pre3.ts (and t/pre2.ts). Chrome: start the mock and settings as above, then bun scratchpad/r10ai/watch.ts pixel.html 7 '.*'.

Suspected cause: A paragraph that opens with 'I have/I've/I left/I kept/I will' or 'Note:' and contains a common word such as version, article, words, text or links counts as the model's own note. Fix lead: count only a note about the task (the Short version, [[B…]], a word count, 'here is the shorter…'), never a paragraph whose words come from the source text.

Skeptic: Confirmed. I rate it medium rather than high. Chrome 9469 with the mock, pixel.html, run twice with the cache cleared in between (1 call each time). The Short version starts with 'The design is almost the same as last year's.' The verdict paragraph 'I have tested every version of the Pixel phone since 2016, and the Pixel 11 is the first one I would not recommend.' is gone, though the original page has it. The offline pre3.ts removes 6 of 7 first-person openers: I have…version, I've…version, I left…article, I kept…words, He mantenido…versión, I will…text. Only 'I cut sugar…' is kept. pre2.ts removes the last-paragraph 'Note: This article was corrected…' and 'Nota: este artículo fue actualizado…', the 'I will write a follow-up article…' close, and 'Here are the links to the forms…'. It also removes the 'I have read every word of the bill's text' op-ed opener. Cause: NOTE_START (text.ts ~656) admits any 'I have/I've/I will/I kept/I left/Note:' opening. NOTE_TOPIC matches everyday words (version, article, text, words, links), so a first-person paragraph about the subject counts as the model's note. A review's verdict or an op-ed's thesis is lost from the Short version and is not put back.

Skeptic's fix lead: src/background/text.ts NOTE_START/NOTE_TOPIC/stripPreamble: count a paragraph as a note only when it talks about the task: [[B…]], a word count, 'here is the shorter/condensed version', 'I kept/removed the placeholders/links/headings', or 'the source/original text'. Drop the bare 'I have/I've/I will/I left/I kept' and 'Note:' openers with generic topic words. Or keep any paragraph most of whose content words appear in the source.

## F1016 (high): The number check still drops faithful warning sentences when the model converts a unit or a ratio ('in a day' to '24 hours', 'one year old' to '12 months', 'nine in ten' to '90%') (rest of F906)

Repro: Offline: cd /home/nico/projects/sift-test-9 && bun /tmp/claude-1001/-home-nico/aac11194-acaf-4dad-a334-0620115b1898/scratchpad/r10ai/t/num2.ts. Chrome: mock and settings as above, then bun scratchpad/r10ai/watch.ts dose.html 7 '.*'.

Suspected cause: Any number in digits that the source does not state in some form drops the whole sentence, and nothing restores it. Cause: unsupported() fails any digit value the source does not state in some form, and dropUnsupported removes the whole sentence with nothing restored. This is the rest of F906. ## F1017 (high): The speaker check drops a correctly attributed lead quote when the source says 'she said' and names the speaker more than 300 characters earlier (rest of F907)

Repro: Offline: cd /home/nico/projects/sift-test-9 && bun /tmp/claude-1001/-home-nico/aac11194-acaf-4dad-a334-0620115b1898/scratchpad/r10ai/t/spk2.ts. Chrome: mock and settings as above, then bun scratchpad/r10ai/watch.ts union.html 7 '.*'.

Suspected cause: With a pronoun attribution in the source, the name must be inside a fixed 300-character window. Fix lead: accept a speaker the source names anywhere before the quote, or on a 'speaker' failure remove only the quote marks or the name instead of the sentence.

Skeptic: Confirmed. I rate it medium-high. Chrome 9469 with the mock, union.html, run twice with a cleared cache. The Short version starts with 'The Northgate plant employs about 2,000 people…'. Whether a correct lead survives depends on how long the paragraphs between are. This is the rest of F907.

Skeptic's fix lead: src/background/text.ts speakerNear: when the source's own attribution is a pronoun, accept a named speaker the source mentions anywhere before the quote (or anywhere in the source).
