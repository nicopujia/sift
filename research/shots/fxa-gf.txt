  p: Gluten-free bread needs a suitable flour blend and different mixing, rising and baking methods from regular bread.
  H2 Which gluten-free flour is best for bread?
  p: No single gluten-free flour works on its own. Use a blend of at least three types to make bread that holds together without being too hard.
  p: A simple mix has:
    li: At least 50% light flour: yellow cornflour, gluten-free oat flour, brown rice flour or white rice flour.
    li: 25% flour rich in protein and fibre, for structure: chickpea (gram), soy, potato or teff flour, or a mix.
    li: 25% starchy flour, to bind the dough: cornflour (also called cornstarch), arrowroot, tapioca or potato starch. Potato starch is different from potato flour, which uses the whole potato.
  p: You can also use a ready-mixed bread flour. Doves Farm bread flour is one option for beginners.
  H2 Can you use yeast in gluten-free bread?
  p: Yes. Yeast is a fungus and is naturally gluten-free. Check dried yeast ingredients for wheat starch. Make sure fresh yeast is fully sealed to prevent contamination with wheat from the bakery.
  p: To avoid yeast, make soda bread using buttermilk and bicarbonate of soda to help it rise.
  H2 Tips for making gluten-free bread
    li: Mix the dough like a batter, until no dry patches remain. Use a food mixer’s regular paddle, not a dough hook. You do not need to knead it.
    li: Shape the mixture and put it on a tray or in a tin.
    li: Gluten-free flour often needs more liquid than wheat flour. Gluten-free bread also needs more yeast because it lacks wheat gluten’s elasticity.
    li: It may take longer to rise, but it only needs one rise. Do not knock it back or give it a second rise.
    li: The mixture is usually wetter and may not hold its shape. Rise it in a boule, mould or loaf tin. For a loaf tin, line it with non-stick baking paper and spray the sides with oil so the loaf is easier to remove.
    li: Gluten-free flour may brown less than wheat flour, so colour alone cannot show when the bread is ready.
    li: Bake until the centre reaches 105C. A digital probe gives the most accurate reading.
    li: Bake at 180C, not the usual 200C. Gluten-free bread takes longer to bake.
    li: Use ingredients at the same temperature. Do not use them straight from the fridge.
    li: Steam at the start of baking can help the bread rise. Add ice cubes to a tray at the bottom of the oven, or spray water just before putting the bread in and closing the door.
    li: Cool the loaf in its tin for 10 minutes, then remove it and cool it fully on a wire rack. Leaving it in the tin can make the base soggy.
    li: Do not store bread in the fridge; the cold draws out moisture.
  H2 Can I use a bread machine?
  p: Yes, if your machine has a gluten-free setting with one rise and no dough knock-back. The programme should mix for 20 minutes, rise for an hour and bake for an hour.
  p: Add the liquid first and the flour on top to prevent dry flour sticking to the pan. After mixing, check that all the flour is mixed in. Use a rubber spatula to mix in any dry parts.
  H2 Can I make it vegan?
  p: Yes, gluten-free bread can be made without egg, but it needs a binder to help the ingredients gel.

  p: Flax seeds, chia seeds, aquafaba (chickpea brine) and agar agar (vegetarian gelatine) can also replace eggs as binders.
