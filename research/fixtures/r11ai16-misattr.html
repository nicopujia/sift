Northgate plant faces job cuts [MISATTR]

Maria Lopez, the head of the workers' union at the Northgate plant, has led the protests since the spring and says the union will fight every job cut the owners announce this year.

The plant employs about 2,000 people in the region and has been a major employer for decades, supplying parts to car makers across Europe and Asia. Its owners have cut shifts twice this year as orders fell.

The plant's director, John Smith, met reporters on Friday. “We cannot keep every job if the orders do not come back,” he said. He added that the company had lost two large customers to rivals abroad.

Workers at the plant held a meeting on Thursday evening in the canteen, where several hundred of them listened to the union's plans and voted to support a strike if the talks fail next week.

Local shops and cafés near the plant say they already feel the cut shifts, because fewer workers stop for lunch or coffee on the way home, and some have shortened their own opening hours.

The regional government has offered to pay for training for workers who lose their jobs, but the union says training alone will not replace the wages that families in the area depend on.

Talks between the union and the owners resume on Monday with a government mediator, and both sides say they hope to reach a deal before the end of the month.
