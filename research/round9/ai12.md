## F907 (high, regression): The speaker check takes the capitalized word nearest a quote as its speaker ('Brazilian', a weekday, 'Videoclip', 'Canción'), so it drops a Short version's lead sentence and rejects Spanish one-liners for 30 days

Found by: R3-ai-background. Where: src/background/text.ts:345-369 (speakerNear), :381 (unsupported); src/background/index.ts:550-557 (a rejected 'about' line is saved as KEEP_TITLE with no TTL)

Evidence: Chrome 9469 with the mock (fx rigged.html, SPEAKER marker). The source says '“It’s a rigged election,” Trump said…'. The model text says 'Trump said on Monday the Brazilian vote was “a rigged election”, without evidence.' The Short version drops that sentence and starts with 'He spoke for over an hour at an Ohio rally…'. That Trump called the vote rigged, the article's main point, is gone. The check picked 'Brazilian' (nearest to the quote), and the source has only 'Brazil'. Offline (scratchpad/r9ai/t/yt.ts), with English titles on a Spanish YouTube page, these return 'speaker': 'Videoclip oficial de «Hopelessly»', 'Videoclip de «Karma Chameleon»' and 'Canción «Billie Jean» de Michael Jackson, video oficial'. Lines that quote no speaker at all are rejected. In 'about' mode that saves KEEP_TITLE for 30 days, so those rows keep their English titles in a Spanish list, the F821 symptom.

Repro: bun /tmp/claude-1001/-home-nico/aac11194-acaf-4dad-a334-0620115b1898/scratchpad/r9ai/t/yt.ts. In Chrome with the mock as above: bun scratchpad/r9ai/watch.ts rigged.html 6.

Suspected cause: NAMEISH counts any capitalized word as a possible speaker: a nationality adjective, a day or month, or a sentence-initial noun in Spanish or English. The check also wants that exact word form near the quote in the source. Fix lead: only test a speaker word that follows or precedes a speech verb (said, dijo, called, calificó), or take the nearest proper name the source has anywhere. Never test a quoted title. On a 'speaker' failure, remove the quote marks instead of dropping the sentence or rejecting the line.

Skeptic: Confirmed, high. Chrome 9469 with the mock, rigged.html. The Short version started with 'He spoke for over an hour at an Ohio rally…'. The sentence 'Trump said on Monday the Brazilian vote was “a rigged election”…' was gone, even though the quote is word for word and Trump is named next to it in the source. Offline, speakerNear picks the capitalized word nearest the quote ('Brazilian'), and the source has only 'Brazil'. Other cases return 'speaker' too: 'Ruiz, the Spanish finance minister, said “we will not raise taxes”' (source says 'Spain's'), 'Videoclip oficial de «Hopelessly»', 'Videoclip de «Karma Chameleon»', 'Canción «Billie Jean» de Michael Jackson, video oficial' ('Canción' is nearer than 'Michael'), 'Tema «Hopelessly», videoclip oficial' and 'Song “Patient A” by Ado'. In doLines (index.ts ~550-557), a line vetLine rejects in about mode is saved as KEEP_TITLE with no TTL, which keeps it 30 days, so the English title stays in a Spanish list. Lines that put a real name next to the title ('Video oficial de Rick Astley para «…»') pass, so only some Spanish rows are hit.

Skeptic's fix lead: src/background/text.ts speakerNear: only test a name tied to a speech verb (said/dijo/calificó/called… right before or after the quote). Or accept the name if it, or a word sharing its stem, appears anywhere in the source. Skip quotes that are titles (« » or “ ” right after a word like video/canción/song, or a quote that matches the item title). In unsupported/dropUnsupported and vetLine, answer a 'speaker' failure by removing the quote marks, not by dropping the sentence or rejecting the line.
